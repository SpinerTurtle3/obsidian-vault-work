1. Know the domain of the team you lead if you want my trust. (Sorry PMs turned EMs -
    
2. Do actual work, not just status updates and networking. It doesnt have to be individual contribution, but there should be a paper trail of process proposals, organizational problem solving, slack messages showing you are actually steering and supporting the team vs reacting and hostage to your calendar (you arent).
    
3. With every decision you make yourself, or communicate from above. Include "why". I need the motivating reason and background context to do my job effectively.
    
4. As a senior, if I come to you with a problem, it means I need your authority or network to unblock me. It doesnt mean i need coaching on how to problem solve or a push to take the initiative myself. I lose trust fastest when I ask for you to influence something but you turn it around in the 1:1 and say something like "well what do you think we should do". I'd rather have a clear "i dont know" or "i dont think I'll succeed at that". Some things really do come down to where you are on the totem pole - especially cross team alignment.
    
5. Give me autonomy to design HOW my work it gets done. I want a goal, the why, and I want to figure out how." given the constraints. If you think I have created something that wont work - tell me the constraint being broken, not how to do it.
    
6. Im ok cancelling 1:1s if neither of us have anything to say and it will become a status update to fill the silence. Despite all the management books saying you should never cancel a 1:1, I may not have much to talk about that week that I havent already brought up immediatly in DMs or slack. Quick slack message to check in before the meeting or cutting the meeting short both work "do you have anything? no, me either, k bye"
    
7. Dont watch my PTO like a hawk - If i am performing, give me some social trust / flexibilty.
    

Especially for more experienced leaders - Its easy to burn out and get into "my job is meetings and alignment" So my most recent frustrations have been around experienced managers who act that way and have no paper trail of the actual work they are doing - slack conversations - meeting notes - fucking anything. Publish a proposal, write a doc showing organizational downsides and how we patch them for now, Publish and revisit team strategy multiple times, etc, etc
